The code in this repository is licensed under [Apache-2.0](LICENSE). **The Apache-2.0 licence
grants rights to the code. It does not grant rights to trademarks** — see section 6 of the
licence text.

"Bingoo", "Bingoo Open", the Bingoo logo and the Bingoo mascot are trademarks of Bingoo.ai.
This page explains what you may do with them, so you do not have to guess.

## You do not need permission to

- **Use the software**, for anything, including commercially. That is what the licence is for.
- **Fork or modify it**, publicly or privately.
- **Say what your software is built on**, factually: "built with Bingoo Open", "compatible with
  Bingoo Open", "a plugin for Bingoo Open". Describing an accurate relationship is ordinary
  descriptive use.
- **Write about the project** — articles, talks, tutorials, reviews, courses — and use the name
  and logo to refer to it.
- **Keep the marks in an unmodified copy.** If you redistribute the project as it is, you do not
  have to strip anything out.

## Please ask first before

- Using "Bingoo" **in the name of your own product, service, company, or domain**, or in a name
  close enough to be confused with it.
- Using the logo or mascot **as the identity of something that is not this project** — your app
  icon, your company mark, your merchandise.
- Anything that suggests your work is **official, endorsed, affiliated, or certified** when it
  is not.
- **Registering** a trademark, domain, or social handle that includes "Bingoo" or a
  confusingly similar term.

Ask at **legal@bingoo.ai**. We would rather say yes to a clear request than argue about an
unclear one.

## If you distribute a modified version

Modified builds must not present themselves as the original. Give your fork its own name and
its own logo, and remove ours from the user interface and package metadata.

You may still state the origin factually — "a fork of Bingoo Open" is fine and welcome. What is
not fine is a user being unable to tell whose software they are running, because that makes our
name answer for code we did not write.

## Why this exists

Open source and trademarks solve different problems. The licence exists so anyone can use,
study, change and share the code. The trademark exists so that when someone sees the name, they
know where the thing came from — which protects users as much as it protects us.

Nothing here is intended to restrict your rights under Apache-2.0. If this policy ever seems to
conflict with the licence, the licence governs the code.
